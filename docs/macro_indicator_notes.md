# Macro Indicator Notes

## 10Y Treasury Yield
The 10Y Treasury Yield is a long-term interest rate benchmark. Investors care because higher long-term yields can pressure equity valuations.

## Fed Funds Rate
The Fed Funds Rate reflects short-term monetary policy. Investors care because it affects borrowing costs, financial conditions, and risk appetite.

## Yield Curve Spread
The yield curve spread measures the difference between long-term and short-term Treasury yields. Investors care because a narrowing or negative spread can signal recession risk or pressure on banks.

## CPI
CPI measures inflation pressure. Investors care because inflation affects rates, purchasing power, margins, and valuation expectations.

## Unemployment Rate
The unemployment rate measures labour market strength or weakness. Investors care because rising unemployment can signal weaker demand and economic stress.

## Consumer Sentiment
Consumer sentiment measures household confidence. Investors care because weaker sentiment can signal softer consumer spending.

## VIX
The VIX measures expected market volatility. Investors care because a rising VIX often signals market fear and risk-off sentiment.

## Credit Spread
Credit spreads measure the extra yield investors demand for holding riskier debt. Investors care because wider spreads can signal tighter financial conditions and higher credit stress.

## Oil Price
Oil prices reflect commodity and energy market conditions. Investors care because oil affects inflation, energy company profits, transport costs, and consumer spending.

## Mortgage Rate
Mortgage rates measure housing-related borrowing costs. Investors care because higher mortgage rates can pressure housing demand, real estate activity, banks, and consumer credit.
